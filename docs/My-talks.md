## RTFM 2.0: Read the Feedback, Make It Better

We've all used RTFM before. What happens though when the users actually follow through? Well, they might have some feedback to offer. User feedback reveals what documentation often misses: assumptions, ambiguity, missing information, and the occasional typo. Whether it’s a nitpick or a dealbreaker, every comment is a clue. This talk outlines how to make feedback part of a continuous improvement process that actually works.

## Fun with Bugs – or How to Better Enjoy Sushi

Every product has bugs, and every product release has bugfixes: a laundry list that users
have been waiting months to read. Bug fixes are like sushi: bite sized in scope, technical
communicators go through them way too fast, and always end up craving more when they’re
finished. That hasn’t been your experience? Clearly you haven’t been having enough fun
with your bugs. This presentation will teach you how, and we’ll also talk about sushi.

## The Tech Writers Troubleshooting Game

Ever wonder when a certain feature was developed and why it wasn't documented? Want to know what (who) caused your content to mysteriously disappear? Who is the all-knowing SME with the hidden information? And, what does this build error even mean?

No need to get frustrated. Come play the tech writer's troubleshooting game. You'll learn how to perform quick diagnoses and calculate the fastest path to any answer you need.
